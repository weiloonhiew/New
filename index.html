Listening

From Alan Sieler's ontological coaching model: listening is not passive reception — it's an active act of interpretation, shaped by mood, body, and a personal and cultural history the listener rarely notices in the moment. A coach's own listening is a skill to design on purpose, not a default to trust blindly.

Language: The Basic Speech Acts

The distinctions ontological coaching draws from speech act theory: every conversation is built from a small set of linguistic acts — assertions, assessments, declarations, offers, promises, and requests — each with its own conditions for landing well. Requests get particular attention here: a request missing one of its structural components is far more likely to be misunderstood, ignored, or quietly resented than fulfilled.

Content and categorization draw on several coaching traditions: the 13-category framework in Brené Brown's Atlas of the Heart; the "moods for action" concept from Ontological Coaching (Echeverría / Newfield), shown in its own 14th category since it treats moods as sustained dispositions rather than momentary emotions; Alan Sieler's ontological coaching model of listening; and the speech-act distinctions (assertions, assessments, declarations, offers, promises, requests) used across that same tradition.
